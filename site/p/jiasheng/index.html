Tang poem · seven-character quatrain唐诗 · 七言绝句

jiǎ贾shēng生

lǐ李shāng商yǐn隐Li Shangyin · Tang唐

Master Jia

 

xuān宣shì室qiú求xián贤fǎng访zhú逐chén臣

jiǎ贾shēng生cái才diào调gèng更wú无lún伦

kě可lián怜yè夜bàn半xū虚qián前xí席

bú不wèn问cāng苍shēng生wèn问guǐ鬼shén神

01

Line by line逐句翻译

  1. 宣室求贤访逐臣Seeking wise men, the emperor called the exiled Jia Yi to the Xuanshi Hall.汉文帝在宣室寻求贤才，召见了被贬的贾谊；
  2. 贾生才调更无伦Jia Yi's talent and grace had no equal.贾谊的才华风度，更是没有人比得上。
  3. 可怜夜半虚前席What a pity: at midnight the emperor leaned forward on his mat, all for nothing,可惜文帝半夜里听得入神，向前挪动坐席，却是白白地挪；
  4. 不问苍生问鬼神for he asked not about the people, only about ghosts and spirits.他不问百姓的生活，只问鬼神的事情。
02

Words字词注释

宣室xuān shì
xuān shì: Xuanshi, the main hall at the front of the Han Weiyang Palace, where the emperor received officials.宣室：汉代未央宫前殿的正室，皇帝在这里接见大臣。
求贤qiú xián
qiú xián: seeking talented people.求贤：寻找有才能的人。
访逐臣fǎng zhú chén
fǎng zhú chén: calling in an exiled minister. Here it means Jia Yi, who had been sent away from court.访逐臣：召见被贬到外地的大臣。逐臣，被赶出朝廷的臣子，这里指贾谊。
贾生jiǎ shēng
jiǎ shēng: Master Jia, Jia Yi, a famous writer and statesman of the Western Han.贾生：贾谊，西汉著名的文学家和政治家。
才调cái diào
cái diào: talent and style.才调：才华和风度。
更无伦gèng wú lún
gèng wú lún: no one could match him. Lún means equals.更无伦：更是没有人比得上。伦，同类，可比的人。
可怜kě lián
kě lián: what a pity.可怜：可惜。
虚前席xū qián xí
xū qián xí: moved forward on his mat in vain. People of old sat on mats, and when they listened closely they leaned in. Xū means for nothing.虚前席：白白地往前挪动坐席。古人坐在席子上，听得入神就不自觉往前凑。虚，白白地。
苍生cāng shēng
cāng shēng: the common people.苍生：百姓。
鬼神guǐ shén
guǐ shén: ghosts and spirits.鬼神：鬼怪和神灵的事。
03

How to read it读音

贾jiǎ
As a surname, this character is read jiǎ.贾：作姓氏读 jiǎ。
调diào
In cái diào, meaning talent and style, the second character is read diào, not tiáo.调：“才调”指才气风度，读 diào，不读 tiáo。
04

Background创作背景

Jia Yi was a brilliant young man of the Western Han. In his twenties he was given high posts by Emperor Wen, but rivals pushed him out, and he was sent away to Changsha. According to the "Records of the Grand Historian", a few years later the emperor called him back and met him in the Xuanshi Hall. The emperor had been thinking about spirits, so he asked Jia Yi where ghosts and spirits come from. They talked until midnight, and the emperor kept leaning forward on his mat without noticing. Li Shangyin wrote this poem from that story. We do not know when.

贾谊是西汉的才子，二十多岁就受到汉文帝重用，后来被排挤，贬到长沙。据《史记》记载，过了几年文帝把他召回来，在宣室接见他。文帝正好在想鬼神的事，就问他鬼神的来历，两人一直谈到半夜，文帝听得不知不觉往前挪坐席。李商隐借这个故事写诗，写作时间不详。

05

The poet作者

Li Shangyin (about 813–about 858) was a great late-Tang poet. With Du Mu he was called "the Little Li and Du". He was very gifted, but all his life he was caught between two warring court factions and never got to use his talent. So writing about Jia Yi, he was also writing about his own frustration of being gifted but unused.

李商隐（约813—约858），晚唐大诗人，和杜牧并称“小李杜”。他很有才华，却一辈子卷在朝廷两派的争斗里，始终没能施展抱负。所以他写贾谊，也是在写自己怀才不遇的苦闷。

06

The mood意境

This poem lifts you up and then lets you down. The first two lines seem to praise: the emperor seeks wise men and calls Jia Yi in himself, and Jia Yi is matchless. We think he will finally be put to good use. Then line three turns with "what a pity". At midnight the emperor leans in, as if he cares deeply. The last line reveals the truth. He does not ask how to run the country or help the people live well. He asks about ghosts. The word "ask" appears twice. "Not asking about the people" and "asking about ghosts" sit side by side, and the contrast is sharp. One line shows both the emperor's foolishness and the waste of a great talent.

这首诗先扬后抑。前两句好像在夸：皇帝求贤，亲自召见；贾谊才华出众，无人能比。读到这里，我们以为贾谊终于要被重用了。第三句“可怜”一转，皇帝半夜听得往前凑，看似很重视。最后一句揭开真相：他问的不是怎样治理国家、让百姓过好日子，而是鬼神。一个“问”字用了两次，“不问苍生”和“问鬼神”放在一起，对比特别强烈。短短一句，就把皇帝的糊涂和人才被浪费的可惜说透了。
