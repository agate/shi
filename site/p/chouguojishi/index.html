chóu酬guō郭jǐ给shì事

wáng王wéi维Wang Wei · Tang唐

A Reply to Secretary Guo

 

dòng洞mén门gāo高gé阁ǎi霭yú余huī晖

táo桃lǐ李yīn阴yīn阴liǔ柳xù絮fēi飞

jìn禁lǐ里shū疏zhōng钟guān官shè舍wǎn晚

shěng省zhōng中tí啼niǎo鸟lì吏rén人xī稀

chén晨yáo摇yù玉pèi佩qū趋jīn金diàn殿

xī夕fèng奉tiān天shū书bài拜suǒ琐wéi闱

qiǎng强yù欲cóng从jūn君wú无nà那lǎo老

jiāng将yīn因wò卧bìng病jiě解cháo朝yī衣

The corpus has a different character for hui (light) in line one; most common editions use the one for sunlight. Both mean the evening glow. In line three the corpus uses an old variant form of shu (sparse); the meaning is the same.

语料首句作“余辉”，通行本多作“余晖”，都是夕阳的光。语料第三句作“疎钟”，“疎”是“疏”的异体字。

01

Line by line逐句翻译

  1. 洞门高阁霭余晖Rows of gates and tall halls are wrapped in the evening glow.一道道宫门和高高的楼阁，笼罩在夕阳的余光里。
  2. 桃李阴阴柳絮飞Peach and plum trees cast thick shade, and willow catkins fly.桃树李树一片浓荫，柳絮在空中飞舞。
  3. 禁里疏钟官舍晚Bells ring now and then in the palace, and evening comes to the offices.宫里传来稀疏的钟声，官署里天色已晚，
  4. 省中啼鸟吏人稀In the Chancellery only birds sing, and few clerks remain.门下省里只有鸟在叫，办事的人都走得差不多了。
  5. 晨摇玉佩趋金殿In the morning, jade pendants swinging, you hurry into the golden hall.您早晨挂着玉佩，快步走进金殿，
  6. 夕奉天书拜琐闱In the evening you carry the emperor's orders back to the Chancellery.傍晚捧着皇帝的诏书，回到门下省。
  7. 强欲从君无那老I would like to follow you, but I cannot help being old,我很想跟着您一起做事，无奈已经老了，
  8. 将因卧病解朝衣so I plan to use my illness as a reason to take off my court robe.打算借着生病，脱下官服辞官回家。
02

Words字词注释

洞门dòng mén
dòng mén: gates opening one after another in a row.洞门：一道接一道相通的门。
霭ǎi
ǎi: to wrap in a soft glow.霭：笼罩。
余晖yú huī
yú huī: the last light of the setting sun.余晖：傍晚太阳的余光。
阴阴yīn yīn
yīn yīn: thick with shade.阴阴：树荫浓密的样子。
柳絮liǔ xù
liǔ xù: willow catkins, seeds with white fluff that float in the wind like cotton.柳絮：柳树的种子，带着白色的绒毛，像棉花一样随风飘。
禁里jìn lǐ
jìn lǐ: inside the palace.禁里：皇宫里。
疏钟shū zhōng
shū zhōng: a bell ringing now and then.疏钟：稀稀落落的钟声。
官舍guān shè
guān shè: the offices.官舍：官员办公的地方。
省中shěng zhōng
shěng zhōng: inside the Chancellery, an important department of the court where secretaries like Guo worked.省中：门下省里。门下省是朝廷的一个重要部门，给事中就在这里做事。
吏人稀lì rén xī
lì rén xī: the clerks have gone home and few are left.吏人稀：办事的小官们都走了，剩下的人很少。
玉佩yù pèi
yù pèi: jade pendants worn by officials, which jingle as they walk.玉佩：官员身上挂的玉饰，走路时会发出响声。
趋qū
qū: to walk with quick, small steps to show respect.趋：小步快走，表示恭敬。
金殿jīn diàn
jīn diàn: the golden hall where the emperor held court.金殿：皇帝上朝的大殿。
奉fèng
fèng: to hold up respectfully.奉：恭敬地捧着。
天书tiān shū
tiān shū: the emperor's written orders.天书：皇帝的诏书。
琐闱suǒ wéi
suǒ wéi: palace gates carved with linked-ring patterns. Here it means the Chancellery.琐闱：刻着连环花纹的宫门，这里指门下省。
强欲qiǎng yù
qiǎng yù: to try hard to want to.强欲：勉强想要。
从君cóng jūn
cóng jūn: to follow you.从君：跟随您。
无那老wú nà lǎo
wú nà lǎo: helpless against old age.无那老：无奈自己老了。
卧病wò bìng
wò bìng: lying ill in bed.卧病：生病躺着。
解朝衣jiě cháo yī
jiě cháo yī: to take off the court robe, meaning to resign.解朝衣：脱下官服，意思是辞官。
03

How to read it读音

舍shè
Here it means a building, an office, so it is read shè, not shě.“官舍”指官署的房子，读 shè，不读 shě。
省shěng
Here it is the name of a government department, read shěng.“省中”的“省”是官署名，读 shěng。
强qiǎng
Here it means forcing oneself, so it is read qiǎng, not qiáng.“强欲”是勉强想要，读 qiǎng，不读 qiáng。
那nà
Wu na means helpless, the same as wu nai. Today it is usually read nà; some read it nuó.“无那”就是“无奈”。现在多读 nà；也有人读 nuó。
朝cháo
Here it means the court, so it is read cháo.“朝衣”是上朝穿的官服，读 cháo。
04

Background创作背景

Secretary Guo was a court secretary surnamed Guo; his full name is no longer known. Such secretaries worked in the Chancellery and checked the emperor's orders. Guo first sent Wang Wei a poem, and Wang Wei wrote this one in reply. The word chou in the title means to answer a poem with a poem. It shows the palace on a spring evening, praises Guo's hard work, and ends by saying the poet is old and wants to resign. It is usually thought to have been written in Wang Wei's later years while he served at court, but the year is not known.

郭给事是一位姓郭的给事中，名字已经不清楚了。给事中是门下省的官员，负责审读诏书。郭给事先写了一首诗送给王维，王维写了这首诗回答他，题目里的“酬”就是用诗回答的意思。诗里写的是春天傍晚的宫廷，夸郭给事勤勉，最后说自己老了，想辞官。一般认为是王维晚年在朝廷做官时写的，具体年份不清楚。

05

The poet作者

Wang Wei (701? to 761) was a great Tang poet and painter, called the Buddha of Poetry. He rose to be a high minister, yet in his heart he always longed for a quiet life in the hills. He was a Buddhist, and in his later years he often stayed at his country house at Wangchuan. The wish to resign at the end of this poem shows exactly that feeling.

王维（701？—761），字摩诘，唐代大诗人、画家，被称为“诗佛”。他做官做到尚书右丞，可心里一直向往山林中安静的生活，信奉佛教，晚年常住在辋川别墅。这首诗最后说想辞官，正是他这种心情的写照。

06

The mood意境

The first two lines are a picture of a spring evening. The gates and halls glow in the sunset, peach and plum trees cast shade, and willow catkins drift. The next two lines show how quiet the palace is. The bell rings now and then, birds sing, and everyone has gone. This quiet also praises Guo: he is honest and careful, so his office has no noise or fuss. Lines five and six show Guo's busy day: into the golden hall in the morning, carrying the emperor's orders in the evening, dignified and hardworking. The last two lines are Wang Wei's own words from the heart: I would like to follow you, but I am old, so I will leave office because of illness. He says it politely, but you can feel he is tired of court life, and his heart has already flown to the hills.

前两句是一幅春天傍晚的画：宫门楼阁染着夕阳，桃李成荫，柳絮飘飞。三四句写宫里的安静：钟声稀稀落落，鸟儿在叫，人都散了。这种安静也在夸郭给事：他做事清廉认真，官署里没有吵闹。五六句写郭给事一天的忙碌，早上进金殿，傍晚捧诏书，又庄重又辛苦。最后两句是王维自己的心里话：我很想跟着您，可是老了，还是借病辞官吧。语气客气，却能感到他对官场已经厌倦，心早已飞向山林了。
