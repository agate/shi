hán寒shí食

hán韩hóng翃Han Hong · Tang唐

Cold Food Day

 

chūn春chéng城wú无chù处bù不fēi飞huā花

hán寒shí食dōng东fēng风yù御liǔ柳xié斜

rì日mù暮hàn汉gōng宫chuán传là蜡zhú烛

qīng轻yān烟sàn散rù入wǔ五hóu侯jiā家

01

Line by line逐句翻译

  1. 春城无处不飞花In Chang'an in spring, blossoms fly everywhere.春天的长安城，处处飘飞着落花。
  2. 寒食东风御柳斜On Cold Food Day the east wind bends the palace willows to one side.寒食节的东风，吹得皇宫里的柳枝都斜向一边。
  3. 日暮汉宫传蜡烛At dusk the palace sends out lit candles,天快黑时，宫里派人传送点燃的蜡烛，
  4. 轻烟散入五侯家and their thin smoke drifts into the homes of the favored lords.淡淡的烟飘散开来，飘进了权贵们的家。
02

Words字词注释

春城chūn chéng
chūn chéng: the city of Chang'an in spring.春城：春天的长安城。
无处不飞花wú chù bù fēi huā
wú chù bù fēi huā: there is no place without flying blossoms. Fēi huā means petals and willow fluff drifting in the wind.无处不飞花：没有一个地方不飘着落花。飞花，随风飘飞的花瓣和柳絮。
寒食hán shí
hán shí: Cold Food Day, a day or two before the Qingming Festival. By custom no fires were lit, and people ate only cold food.寒食：寒食节，在清明节前一两天。按照风俗，这几天不能生火做饭，只吃冷的食物。
御柳yù liǔ
yù liǔ: the willows of the imperial palace.御柳：皇宫里的柳树。
斜xié
xié: bent to one side by the wind.斜：被风吹得歪向一边。
汉宫hàn gōng
hàn gōng: the Han palace, used to mean the Tang palace. Tang poets often wrote about their own time this way.汉宫：借汉朝的皇宫来指唐朝的皇宫。唐诗里常用这种写法。
传蜡烛chuán là zhú
chuán là zhú: passing out candles. Fire was banned in the city on Cold Food Day, but at dusk the emperor sent lit candles to his favorite officials as a special honor.传蜡烛：寒食节全城不许生火，到了傍晚，皇帝却派人把点燃的蜡烛传送给受宠的大臣，表示恩宠。
轻烟qīng yān
qīng yān: the faint smoke from the candles.轻烟：蜡烛冒出的淡淡的烟。
五侯wǔ hóu
wǔ hóu: the five lords, meaning the powerful families the emperor favored. Some say it refers to five eunuchs of the Eastern Han who were made lords on the same day; others say five uncles of a Western Han emperor.五侯：泛指受皇帝宠信的权贵人家。一说指东汉桓帝同一天封侯的五个宦官，一说指西汉成帝同一天封侯的五个舅舅。
03

How to read it读音

处chù
Here it means a place, so it is read chù.这里指地方，读 chù。
斜xié
Textbooks today read it xié. The old reading was xiá, which rhymes with huā and jiā.现在课本读 xié。古音读 xiá，和“花、家”押韵。
04

Background创作背景

Cold Food Day falls a day or two before Qingming. By custom, fires were put out and people ate only cold food. This poem shows the holiday in Chang'an, the Tang capital. It is usually placed in the reigns of Emperor Daizong or Dezong, but the exact year is not known. A Tang book of poem stories by Meng Qi says Emperor Dezong liked this poem so much that he gave Han Hong a post writing the emperor's orders.

寒食节在清明前一两天，按风俗要禁火，只吃冷食。这首诗写的就是唐朝都城长安过寒食节的景象，一般认为写于唐代宗、唐德宗时，具体年份说不清。据唐人孟棨的《本事诗》记载，唐德宗很欣赏这首诗，因此提拔韩翃做了替皇帝起草诏书的官。

05

The poet作者

Han Hong's birth and death years are not known. He came from Nanyang, in today's Henan, and passed the top exam in the Tianbao years of Emperor Xuanzong. He was one of the Ten Talents of the Dali Era, a group of well-known poets of that time. Most of his poems are about farewells or were written in reply to friends, in rich, elegant words. This poem is his most famous.

韩翃，生卒年不详，字君平，南阳（今属河南）人，唐玄宗天宝年间考中进士。他是“大历十才子”之一，这是唐代宗大历年间一群有名的诗人。他的诗多写送别和唱和，用词华美，这首《寒食》是他最有名的作品。

06

The mood意境

The first two lines fill the city with spring. Petals and willow fluff fly everywhere, and the east wind sways the palace willows. "No place without flying blossoms" uses two negatives to mean "blossoms fly everywhere", which sounds even stronger. The last two lines turn to dusk. No one in the city may light a fire, yet candles go out from the palace, and their smoke drifts into the homes of the favored lords. On the surface this is just the bustle of the holiday and the emperor's kindness. But some readers hear a quiet complaint: ordinary homes stay cold and dark, while the powerful enjoy special treatment. Others think the poet simply paints a peaceful time, with no complaint at all. Either way the picture is lovely: flying blossoms, slanting willows, and thin smoke drifting in the evening light.

前两句写满城春色：花瓣和柳絮到处飞，东风吹着宫里的柳枝。“无处不飞花”用两个否定词，说的是“处处都在飞花”，语气更加强烈。后两句写傍晚：全城都不许点火，只有皇宫传出的蜡烛，把烟送进了五侯家里。这几句看上去只是写节日的热闹和皇帝的恩宠，可是有人读出了讽刺：普通人家冷冷清清，受宠的权贵却能得到特权。一说诗人只是在描写太平景象，并没有讽刺的意思。不管怎样理解，画面都很美：漫天飞花、斜斜的柳枝、暮色里飘散的轻烟。
